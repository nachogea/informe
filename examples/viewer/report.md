# A calmer operating rhythm

**Decision brief · Illustrative product operations report**

The team should replace its daily status meeting with a short written update and two focused weekly reviews. The objective is to protect time for execution while retaining clear ownership of decisions and risks.

## The decision

Run a **four-week pilot** with one product team. Keep incident response and urgent escalation unchanged. Measure time spent in meetings, clarity of ownership, and the time required to unblock work.

> The pilot succeeds when coordination improves without moving the same meeting load into chat.

## What changes

- **Monday:** publish the week's outcomes, owners, and dependencies.
- **Wednesday:** hold a 25-minute unblock review focused on decisions.
- **Friday:** summarize outcomes and carry forward unresolved risks.
- **Every day:** post a concise asynchronous update only when something materially changes.

## How we evaluate it

| Signal | Baseline | Pilot target |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Scheduled coordination | 150 minutes / week | Under 60 minutes / week |
| Decision ownership | Often implicit | Named in every decision |
| Urgent blockers | Discovered in stand-up | Escalated when discovered |

These are proposed targets, not measured outcomes. Collect the baseline before starting and review the evidence after two weeks.

## Risks and safeguards

A written process can disadvantage people who lack context. Give every update a short explanation of why the work matters, link the relevant document, and name the person who can clarify the next step.

1. Keep the update template short enough to complete in five minutes.
2. Rotate the facilitator to avoid creating a coordination bottleneck.
3. Review whether questions go unanswered for more than one working day.

## Next steps

The team lead should choose the pilot team, capture the baseline, and publish the working agreement. At the end of four weeks, decide whether to adopt, adjust, or stop the process.

See the [working agreement section](#what-changes) before the kickoff. This report uses illustrative assumptions and contains no live operational data.
